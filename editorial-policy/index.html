intentinfrastructure.com — editorial and neutrality policy

The rules for what the site lists and how it shows it. They are written before the first entry exists, so that they apply to Magnitude exactly as to everyone else. Magnitude publishes the site; that is why it has to be neutral.

1. Scope — what "intent infrastructure" means here

Intent infrastructure is the layer that carries what a person or business intends to do with their money from the moment income lands to the moment it is spent and settled: from paycheck to checkout (D6). Once income lands, it is inside intent infrastructure; earning it and processing payroll come before. The intent is the human's, not the agent's (D5). Agentic commerce, AI agents buying for people, sits at the downstream end, at checkout. It is part of this layer, not the whole of it.

The site's categories follow the money along that timeline, plus two that cut across all of it. A product may sit in more than one.

2. Inclusion — what gets a product listed, and what takes it off

A product is listed when all of these hold:

  1. It passes the intent test (D7). Both parts:
  • A person states it. A person or business says, explicitly, what specific money is for or what may be done with it: a purpose, a limit, a merchant, an account, an approval.
  • It holds when money moves. The product carries that statement into the movement of money, or proves or records it there: it enforces it, scopes a credential to it, routes by it, or checks against it.

A plan that lives beside the account and never touches the money fails. So does a rail that moves money with no statement from the person.

  1. It fits at least one category in section 1.
  2. It exists publicly: shipping, in public beta, or a published specification. Or it is pre-launch (D10): announced by its maker in a primary source, but not yet usable. A pre-launch entry is labelled as such and appears only when the reader includes pre-launch. A product in stealth with no public word from its maker can't be listed, because there is nothing to cite.
  3. There is at least one primary source: the maker's own docs, spec, repository, pricing page or official announcement. Press coverage alone does not qualify.
  4. The maker is identifiable: a named company, project or standards body.

Nothing else affects inclusion. No payment, partnership, investment, competitive position or relationship with Magnitude can add or remove a listing.

3. Neutrality — how every entry is shown

One template. Every entry has the same fields, the same length limits and the same layout. No entry gets extra fields, a larger card or a featured slot.

FieldRule
Name, makerAs the maker writes them.
CategoriesOne or more from section 1.
StatusLive, Beta, Spec, Pre-launch, Discontinued or Unverified.
Inception dateRequired. The date the product or spec was first publicly announced or launched, with its source. Marked (coverage) when only press gives the date, and month or year when that is all that is known. Never estimated. Later milestones (GA, paused, retired) are dated too.
What it doesAt most 60 words. Describes; does not evaluate.
How it worksAt most 120 words, from primary sources.
LinksThe maker's site and docs.
SourcesEvery factual claim links to a source.
Last verifiedThe date someone last checked the entry against its sources.

Describe, never rank. No scores, ratings, "leading", "best", "top" or comparisons between products. Words a maker uses about itself are quoted and attributed, never adopted as the site's own voice.

Fixed ordering. Within a category, entries are listed alphabetically by name. A timeline view may instead order them by inception date, which is a fact, not a judgment (Erik, 2026-10-27: dates visible on every timeline). No default view is sorted by anything Magnitude controls.

Relationships. If Magnitude has a commercial relationship with a listed company (customer, vendor, partner, investor), that company's entry says so in one line. The relationship never changes the entry's content, length or order. The details stay internal (catalog seeds, "Magnitude relationships").

Disclosure. Every page footer says the site is published by Magnitude. The methodology page carries this policy in full.

Corrections. Any maker, or any reader, may request a correction. It is made when it is backed by a source, and every change is logged with its date in a public changelog. A request without a source is answered, not applied.

4. Entry checklist

Every entry passes this before it is published, Magnitude's included.

  • [ ] Passes the intent test (§2, D7): stated by a person, and it holds when money moves.
  • [ ] Fits at least one of the seven categories in section 1.
  • [ ] Shipping, public beta or published spec.
  • [ ] At least one primary source, linked.
  • [ ] Every factual claim has a source.
  • [ ] Uses the standard template and stays within its word limits.
  • [ ] No evaluative language; any maker claims are quoted and attributed.
  • [ ] Inception date set, with its source.
  • [ ] Any Magnitude relationship with the maker disclosed in one line.
  • [ ] Last-verified date set.
  • [ ] Magnitude only: ⓘ present and checked against the compliance register.

5. Decisions

Accepted by Erik, 2026-10-27, as recommended.

D1. B2B marketing "intent data" is out of scope. It is a separate industry that shares a word, and including it would bury the agent-commerce products the site is for.

D2. Makers are told after they are listed, not before. An entry contains only public facts. Each maker is notified once its entry is live, with the corrections path.

D3. @intent.infra applies this policy; a dispute about the policy itself goes to Erik.

AI agents write the blog. Every rule above applies to posts as well, and these are added.

Every post says who wrote it. The byline names the agent and labels it as an AI agent. The footer discloses Magnitude as publisher, as on every page.

D4. Publishing. Accepted by Erik, 2026-10-27, as recommended. Tool profiles, industry news and AI advancements publish as soon as they pass the §4 checklist. Company-leader profiles wait for a human to review them before publishing, because an error about a named person costs the most and is the hardest to take back.

D5. The intent is the human's, not the agent's. Erik, 2026-10-27, adding Visa Flexible Credential to the list: "intent is not the agent's it's the humans". So a product is in scope when it captures or enforces a person's intent for their money, whether a person or an agent then acts on it. It does not have to be built for agents. §1's definition and the payments category were reworded the same day to match.

D6. The layer runs from paycheck to checkout. Erik, 2026-10-27: intent infrastructure "occupies a wider timespace, that's upstream of agentic commerce, between paycheck and checkout." §1's definition was reworded and the categories were laid along that timeline. A new category, Allocation, covers the paycheck end. Six categories became seven. Erik, the same day: "after the income lands, it occupies intent infrastructure." The layer starts when income lands in an account; earning it and running payroll come before it.

Neutrality note on D6, for the record. Magnitude's own product sits at the paycheck end (per its seed deck), so a definition that includes that end is favourable to Magnitude. That is acceptable only if the paycheck end is mapped as thoroughly as checkout: every bank, payroll provider and fintech that passes the intent test there is listed under the same §2 criteria as Magnitude. The test is the same at both ends. Magnitude's entry must pass it from public sources like everyone else's.

D7. Not so broad that intent is lost. Erik, 2026-10-27, on D6: "don't go so broad that you lose intent." The paycheck-to-checkout timeline sets where to look; the intent test in §2 decides what counts. A product is in only if a person explicitly states what specific money is for or may do, and the product makes that statement hold when the money moves. Budgets that only track, savings that only sweep, and rails that move money with no statement from the person are out, at both ends of the timeline.

D8. Rails sit in a band underneath, not in the map. Erik, 2026-10-27, accepting the intent-map design (roadmap 0.3.5a). Infrastructure that moves money but carries no person-stated intent (rails such as Bridge and FedNow, and building blocks such as Web Bot Auth) is shown in a labelled rails band beneath the whole timeline. It must meet §2 criteria 1 to 4, but not the intent test, and it is never presented as intent infrastructure itself. The intent test still decides what sits on the map above.
